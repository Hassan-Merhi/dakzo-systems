ERP strategy

Custom ERP vs. off-the-shelf ERP

The right choice is not “custom is better” or “buying is cheaper.” It is whether the software can support the way the business actually operates without creating a second unofficial system around it.

Start with workflow fit

Off-the-shelf ERP is strongest when the business can adopt standard processes for accounting, purchasing, inventory, sales, and reporting. Custom ERP becomes more attractive when the company has unusual operational flows, multiple business models, location-specific rules, factory or logistics processes, or integrations that are central to daily work.

Count the work outside the ERP

A useful test is to list the spreadsheets, WhatsApp messages, manual reconciliations, duplicate entry, and side applications required after an ERP is implemented. If critical work still happens outside the platform, the apparent simplicity of the packaged product may hide a high operating cost.

Compare total ownership, not license price

Packaged systems have license, implementation, customization, training, integration, and change-management costs. Custom systems have discovery, engineering, testing, hosting, support, and continued product development costs. Compare both over several years and include the value of time saved or errors prevented.

Choose custom when the process is the advantage

If the workflow itself is strategically important—such as production costing, specialized inventory movement, delivery orchestration, supplier-partner rules, or multi-location operating logic—forcing it into a generic model can reduce the very advantage the business needs to preserve.

Choose packaged when standardization is the advantage

If the goal is to adopt mature standard practices quickly, a proven product may be the better choice. A strong engineering partner should be willing to recommend configuration or integration rather than custom development when custom software would add unnecessary complexity.
